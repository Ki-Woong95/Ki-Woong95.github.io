I am a **Ph.D. candidate in Linguistics** at the **University of Arizona**, specializing in **Phonetics** and **Computational Linguistics**.  I expect to complete my Ph.D. in May 2027. I also earned an **M.S. in Human Language Technology** at the University of Arizona in 2025.

My research asks how humans and computational models process variable speech and recognize when the available information is insufficient for a reliable interpretation. For example, the word *just* can occur in very different reduced forms in spontaneous conversation, yet listeners often recognize it successfully.

In **phonetics**, I study how speech reduction varies with surrounding sounds and how listeners use acoustic cues to recognize reduced words. In **computational linguistics**, I investigate speech representations, automatic speech recognition (ASR) of spontaneous speech, and answerability monitoring in speech-reasoning models.

### Research at a glance

- **Speech production and perception:** Phonetic reduction in conversational speech, especially context-dependent realizations of *just* and listeners' responses to reduced forms.
- **Speech representations and ASR:** Whether explicit prosodic information benefits recognition beyond additional trainable model capacity.
- **Reliable speech reasoning:** Whether models can detect insufficient evidence during reasoning and reassess potentially unsupported answers.

My longer-term research interests include comparing human and machine recognition of **spontaneous speech** and developing speech-language models that respond adaptively to different sources of uncertainty.


### Teaching and research experience

At the University of Arizona, I have been the instructor of record for **Computational Linguistics (LING 438)**, **Statistical Natural Language Processing (LING 439)**, and **Introduction to Linguistics (LING 201)**. I have also worked as a graduate research assistant in the Douglass Phonetics Lab and as a speech-technology research intern at XRI Global, working with ASR for low-resource languages.
