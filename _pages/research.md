My research connects **phonetics** and **computational linguistics**, with a focus on speech variability, spoken-word recognition, and the reliability of speech-language models. I combine acoustic and perceptual studies with computational analyses of speech representations.

## Research areas

### Speech production and perception

I investigate how words change in spontaneous speech and how listeners recover words from reduced forms. My work on the English word *just* examines how neighboring sounds—particularly sibilants—and lexical predictability are associated with reduction. An ongoing perception study asks how listeners use sibilant and vowel duration across phonetic contexts.

**Related work:** 

[Moon & Warner (2025), *Proceedings of Meetings on Acoustics*](https://doi.org/10.1121/2.0002080) 

Perception study (accepted poster at the 191st ASA Meeting (2026))

### Spontaneous-speech ASR and speech representations

I study how computational models represent acoustic and phonetic variability. In a parameter-matched study using frozen HuBERT, adding a trainable fusion pathway improved recognition of spontaneous speech, but learned prosodic features did not yield a significant incremental gain over a capacity-matched control. Related work examines where phonetic variation becomes accessible across model layers.

**Related work:** 

[Moon & Brenner, submitted to ICASSP 2027 (arXiv preprint)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36754) 

Moon & Warner, submitted to ICPhS 2027

### Uncertainty and speech reasoning

My ongoing research investigates whether intermediate representations in speech-reasoning models distinguish answerable and unanswerable inputs. I study lightweight monitoring mechanisms that prompt models to reassess potentially unsupported answers during generation. The goal is to understand when a model has enough evidence to answer and how it can respond when the available audio is insufficient.

**Status:** Ongoing research; no publication is claimed here.

## Open research data

As a graduate research assistant in the **Douglass Phonetics Lab**, I curated previously collected Dutch and English speech-perception data into two publicly released datasets comprising approximately one million human phonetic-identification responses.

- **Dutch speech-perception dataset:** [DOI: 10.25422/azu.data.21498258.v1](https://doi.org/10.25422/azu.data.21498258.v1)
- **English speech-perception dataset:** [DOI: 10.25422/azu.data.21498210.v1](https://doi.org/10.25422/azu.data.21498210.v1)

The datasets are hosted by the University of Arizona Research Data Repository. This work involved organizing previously collected responses, stimuli, and supporting documentation for public release.
